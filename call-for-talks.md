We're looking for speakers! Whether you're a seasoned presenter or it's your first time in front of a crowd, BAWSUG wants to hear from you. Any tech talk is welcome — cloud, security, DevOps, AI/ML, infrastructure, automation, you name it.

### A quick note on how we pick talks

BAWSUG is a **community-first** group. That means talks from people in the community — folks building things, solving problems, and learning in the trenches — will always come first. Always. Even over talks delivered by AWS themselves.

We know vendors have great things to share, and we're not closing the door on that. If you're a vendor and you have something genuinely useful to present, we'll absolutely consider it. But we're never going to bump a community member's talk to make room for a product pitch. That's just not who we are.

So if you've been on the fence about submitting — don't be. Your story matters more here than any polished keynote.

---

We have two formats to fit your story:

---

## "This Is How I Built This" — Full Presentations

**~45-60 minutes** | Ongoing, year-round

Got a project you're proud of? A problem you cracked? A stack you wrangled into submission? Tell us about it.

These are hands-on, real-world talks about **how you solved a problem using cloud technologies**. It doesn't have to be enterprise-scale — personal projects are just as welcome.

**What we're looking for:**

- How you architected and built a solution on AWS (or multi-cloud)
- Lessons learned, tradeoffs, and things you'd do differently
- Live demos, diagrams, war stories — all fair game

**Examples:**

- "How we migrated a monolith to ECS and lived to tell the tale"
- "I built a serverless pipeline to automate my veggie garden"
- "Securing a multi-account org at a Fortune 500 — what actually worked"

The only requirement: you built something, and you can walk us through it.

---

## Lightning Talks — Summer Sessions

**5-7 minutes each** | Summer 2026

Don't have enough material for a full hour? Perfect. Lightning talks are short, focused presentations that get straight to the point.

This format is ideal for:

- A quick demo of a tool or service you've been using
- A single insight or technique worth sharing
- A problem you ran into and how you fixed it
- An idea you're exploring and want feedback on

**Tips for a great lightning talk:**

- Pick 2-3 core points — that's all you need
- Open with a hook that grabs attention
- Show, don't tell — use visuals over bullet points
- Don't rush. Cut content before you speed up delivery
- End with a call to action or a question for the group

Lightning talks are a great way to get your feet wet with presenting, spark conversations, and share something you're excited about — all in under 7 minutes.

*For more on the lightning talk format, check out [USC's Lightning Talks guide](https://libguides.usc.edu/LightningTalks).*
